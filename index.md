\### I. Principal_i = How Something  

Two foundational principals (constraints) governed the system for millennia:



- \*\*i=1 Newtonian / energy (muscle) costs\*\*: Nearly all work depended on human and animal muscle (a person ≈ one old light bulb; a horse ≈ 7×). Water wheels and sails added little. These principals set the resource horizon: progress was possible in principle but trapped by non-scalable energy and non-transferable, non-correctable knowledge.

The world was sampled slowly and locally; representations stayed fragile and unshared.

- Invention itself becomes an industry; progress starts manufacturing its own accelerators (printing presses, engines, labs, computers, AI).  



Gaps between major breakthroughs shrink dramatically: thousands of years → centuries → decades → years. The same positive loop that began with Neolithic settlement (more people → more ideas → better preservation) now runs on machines rather than muscle.

The key move is to treat the video not simply as a history of technological progress, but as a theogony of successive principals: each generation inherits a world shaped by the previous generation’s surviving inventions, then discovers that its own binding constraints are different.

Your two initial principals are well chosen:

- \\(i=1\\): Newtonian costs — the physical cost of moving matter, producing energy, and doing work.
- \\(i=2\\): Epistemic costs — the cost of making knowledge visible, transferable, verifiable, and reusable.

The deeper claim is that human progress accelerated when energy and knowledge became independently scalable. The next question is what happens when those two constraints are no longer the dominant ones.

## The pentad: how progress reproduces itself

## I

Principalᵢ — How Something

The binding constraint; what the world makes costly.

- \\(i=1\\): Newtonian costs — energy, force, labor, transport.
- \\(i=2\\): Epistemic costs — knowledge acquisition, transmission, verification.

## II

Brokerᵢⱼ — Becomes Visible

The medium that samples and represents the constraint.

Muscle, manuscripts, printing presses, scientific journals, electrical grids, networks, and eventually machine-readable data.

## III

Agentᵢⱼₖ & Gametesᵢⱼₖ — Acquires Significance

Agents recombine inherited components into new capabilities.

Gutenberg recombines existing printing techniques; Watt improves the steam engine; Edison organizes invention as a repeatable institutional process. Each innovation supplies material for subsequent innovations.

## IV

Collision — Rises in Priority

Principal, broker, agent, and gamete interact; consequences compete for survival.

Cheap energy without scalable knowledge produces one trajectory. Scalable knowledge without cheap energy produces another. Their interaction changes which innovations become viable, valuable, and reproducible.

## V

Selection — Graveyard & Survivors → I′

What persists changes the conditions of the next generation.

Failed technologies, forgotten techniques, displaced institutions, and surviving innovations determine the inherited landscape. Survivors become the next generation's infrastructure; the next principal emerges from the constraints that remain.

## V. What should the missing term be?

My choice is Selection → Inheritance → New Principals.

The graveyard matters because progress is not the accumulation of everything humanity invents. Most possible combinations never become consequential; others flourish briefly and disappear. What survives is inherited unevenly, and that inheritance changes the next round of possibilities.

The recursion is:

\\[ I \rightarrow II \rightarrow III \rightarrow IV \rightarrow V \rightarrow I' \\]

And crucially, \\(I'\\) is not merely \\(I\\) with more technology. It is a changed problem.
